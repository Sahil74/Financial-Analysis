This diagram explains how a business calculates its Gross Profit and Operating Profit. These are two important concepts in accounting and data analytics, especially when you're analysing a company's financial performance.


1. Revenue − Direct Costs = Gross Profit

Revenue - Money earned from selling products.
$10,000

Direct Costs - Cost of buying or making the products sold.
$4,000

Gross Profit - What remains after direct costs.
$6,000

\[
\$10,000-\$4,000=\$6,000
\]


Imagine you sell 200 T-shirts for $50 each.
- Revenue = 200 × $50 = $10,000
- Direct costs = buying those 200 T-shirts costs $4,000.
- Gross profit = $10,000 − $4,000 = $6,000
Notice that gross profit is not necessarily the money you keep. You still have other business expenses to pay.


2. Gross Profit − Operating Expenses = Operating Profit

Now, imagine the business also has these monthly expenses:

Operating expense
Shop rent	$1,000
Employee wages	$2,000
Advertising	$500
Electricity and other running costs	$300
Total operating expenses	$3,800

Let's calculate the operating profit.
GROSS PROFIT
$6,000
−
OPERATING EXPENSES
$3,800

OPERATING PROFIT
$2,200
\[
\$6,000-\$3,800=\$2,200
\]


The business has $2,200 in operating profit after paying its direct costs and operating expenses.

Operating profit measures how much the business earns from its normal operations before accounting for items such as interest and income tax.

- Revenue: Which products generate the most sales?
- Gross profit: Which products are profitable after their direct costs?
- Operating profit: Is the business earning enough after its running expenses?
- Profit margin: How much profit does the business make for every dollar of revenue?

For example, in this scenario:
Metric	Calculation	Result
Gross profit margin	$6,000 ÷ $10,000 × 100	60%
Operating profit margin	$2,200 ÷ $10,000 × 100	22%

A 22% operating profit margin means the business generates 22 cents in operating profit for every $1 of revenue.

One final distinction: operating profit is not always the same as net profit. 
Interest, taxes and other non-operating items may still need to be accounted for.

The easiest way to remember it:
- Revenue = money coming in from sales.
- Gross profit = sales money left after the direct costs of the products sold.
- Operating profit = gross profit left after the expenses of running the business.
- Net profit is generally what remains after subtracting taxes and other expenses from operating profit. (Net Profit = Profit Before Tax - Income Tax)
